What the capabilities mean
MIT open source
The platform itself is MIT licensed. Refine’s license does not change the licenses or service terms of your agents and models.
Local repositories & runtime
Run the coordinator alongside repositories you control. Refine uses your existing projects and Git history; model hosting depends on your agent.
Use installed coding agents
Refine supervises supported coding-agent CLIs, including Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, and Copilot. Choosing an AI model alone is a different capability.
Delivery & review steps
Refine retains Goals and Rounds through Plan, Implement, Quality, Governance, and Review. Edit Skills and event bindings to configure the work at each step.
Coordinate across machines
Refine coordinates work across registered nodes. This row also counts distributed workflow workers; it does not imply identical fleet behavior.
Isolated work & recovery evidence
Refine uses a separate Git worktree for Goal work and retains failed attempts, logs, and candidate context for an explicit recovery decision.
Product qualifications & sources

Sources checked September 15, 2026. These are readings of published capabilities, not performance tests. “Not documented” means the sources below do not establish that capability; it does not mean the product cannot do it. “Custom setup” means integration or additional configuration is needed.

  • Refine: see the Refine Hub and source and MIT license. Built-in capabilities still require installation, agent authentication, and node configuration.
  • Direct agents: a category of independently operated coding agents, rather than one platform. Licensing, local or hosted execution, review, isolation, and recovery depend on the agent and your setup; no universal product rating is implied.
  • Blitzy: its product, deployment options, and FAQ describe proprietary technology, existing-codebase development, editable specification approval, validation, and team handoff. Customer VPC and custom on-premises deployments are available by engagement. Its own specialized agents are documented; supervising installed coding-agent CLIs, coordinating your separate machines, and retaining isolated Git work for recovery are not established there.
  • n8n: the product and license overview describes a source-available platform under Sustainable Use and Enterprise licenses, with self-hosting, integrations, and human approvals. Queue mode distributes executions across workers. We classify repository work, installed coding agents, software delivery steps, and Git isolation with recovery evidence as custom setup: they can be assembled with workflow logic and tools such as Execute Command, rather than an equivalent documented delivery lifecycle. That node runs on self-hosted instances, is disabled by default, and is unavailable on n8n Cloud.

What Refine does

Your team's agentic software delivery system.

01

Empower your team

Extend product feedback and app edits to your whole team. Keep planning, features, Goals, chat, and human verification in one workflow. Enter, success, error, and exit events connect your Skills; shared controls let you recover with context when a step fails. Failed work waits for an explicit decision. Manage Governance, Quality, and shared guidance through Skills.

02

Agent fleet

Orchestrate your agent fleet across decentralized repositories, branches, processes, and your fleet of nodes. Connect any MCP client to the always-on local endpoint, and cheap feedback loops keep agent work moving while git keeps everyone in sync.

03

Governance

Keep agent work aligned with your product intent, local rules, and requirements. Review stays grounded before changes merge.

04

Hub

Keep reports, historical records, and static sites with your app's state. Manage them from Hubs in the left rail, the CLI, API, or MCP, and publish reports as websites hosted by Refine.
